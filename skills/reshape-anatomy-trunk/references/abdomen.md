# Abdomen and flanks

## Visual references

- [OpenStax Figure 11.16, abdominal muscles](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-4-axial-muscles-of-the-abdominal-wall-and-thorax). Trace muscle direction and layering between costal margin and pelvis.
- [OpenStax Figure 7.32, thoracic cage](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/7-4-the-thoracic-cage). Use the costal margin as the superior boundary.

Use the figures to locate structures. Compare external forms and pose against the subject reference.

## Modeling

Anchor the abdomen between the costal margin and pelvis. Integrate rectus abdominis, obliques, and linea alba into a continuous surface beneath the tissue thickness indicated by the target.

## Evidence to capture

Compare front and three-quarter captures with the belly and flank in view. If the pose bends, verify continuous compression and extension rather than a fixed engraved pattern.

## Review and rejection

Inspect profile and three-quarter views at rest and in flexion. Check whether surface relief, navel, and flanks respond to the pose.

Reject these defects when they conflict with the documented target. A grid of attached muscle blocks or a navel disconnected from the abdominal wall.
