# Shoulder and axilla

Follow the subject, pose, and style reference for form, variation, and asymmetry. Record landmarks and views for measurements. Use population averages as context and mark hidden or inferred regions.

## Visual references

- [OpenStax Figures 8.3 and 8.4, shoulder girdle](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-1-the-pectoral-girdle). Identify the acromion and the clavicle connection.
- [OpenStax Figure 11.23, muscles that move the humerus](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-5-muscles-of-the-pectoral-girdle-and-upper-limbs). Study the deltoid and transitions to the anterior and posterior axillary walls.

Use the figures to locate structures. Compare external forms and pose against the subject reference.

## Modeling

Use clavicle, acromion, and scapula to seat the humeral head. Place the deltoid over the joint and read the front and rear axillary folds against the thorax.

## Evidence to capture

Compare matching front and rear three-quarter crops with the arm down and raised where required. Check the axillary gap and continuity of both folds.

## Review and rejection

Check front and back with the arm down and abducted where applicable. Inspect the space and folds beneath the shoulder.

Reject these defects when they conflict with the documented target. A ball pasted onto the torso, a filled-in axilla, or a hollow that tears when the arm lifts.

Apply the saved-file review in [ReShape](../reshape/SKILL.md). Record the view and discrepancy, correct the cause, and recapture. Missing evidence for a required criterion blocks regional approval.
